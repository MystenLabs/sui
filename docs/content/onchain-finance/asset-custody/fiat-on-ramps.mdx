Fiat on-ramp providers accept traditional currency payments (credit cards, bank transfers, mobile wallets) and deliver Sui-native tokens to a user's wallet address. You embed an on-ramp provider into your app so users can purchase Sui assets without leaving your interface.

Multiple third-party providers offer Sui on-ramp integration with varying asset support, regional coverage, and payment methods. You choose providers based on the regions you serve, the assets your users need, and the payment methods your audience prefers.

## Direct providers and aggregators

On-ramp providers fall into 2 categories:

- **Direct providers:** Banxa, Unlimit, Bridge.xyz, Stripe, Coinbase, Alchemy Pay, OnRamp.money, CoinVoyage, and Bungee connect directly to payment rails and process transactions themselves. You integrate their SDK or API and manage the provider relationship.
- **Aggregators:** Onramper and Meld route transactions through multiple underlying providers (for example, Onramper routes through Kraken, Topper, Banxa, and MoonPay for Sui on-ramps). Aggregators simplify integration because a single SDK covers multiple providers and regions.

:::info

Aggregators like Meld and Onramper route transactions through multiple underlying providers. This broadens regional and asset coverage but means your actual payment experience depends on which sub-provider handles a given transaction.

:::

## Choosing a provider

Select an on-ramp provider based on 4 criteria:

1. **Regional coverage:** Match the provider's supported countries to where your users are located.
2. **Asset support:** Verify that the provider supports the specific Sui assets your app uses (SUI, USDC, WAL, DEEP, and others). Asset support changes frequently, so confirm with the provider before launching.
3. **Payment methods:** Consider which fiat payment methods your users prefer (cards, bank transfers, mobile wallets). Coverage varies by region.
4. **Integration complexity:** Direct provider integrations give you more control but require managing multiple relationships for broad coverage. Aggregators offer a single integration point across multiple providers and regions.

:::tip

If you need both broad regional coverage and multiple payment methods, consider integrating an aggregator (Meld or Onramper) alongside a direct provider that covers your primary market. This approach gives you fallback coverage without the complexity of managing many individual provider integrations.

:::

## Security considerations

When you integrate a fiat on-ramp into your app, follow these practices:

- **Verify transaction intent before signing.** Display clear, human-readable descriptions of what each ramp transaction does before the user approves it. Avoid blind signing of opaque transaction bytes ([Security Best Practices](https://docs.sui.io/develop/security/best-practices)).
- **Validate provider package IDs.** If a ramp provider uses onchain smart contracts, verify their package addresses at runtime. Do not trust contract addresses based on names alone ([Security Best Practices](https://docs.sui.io/develop/security/best-practices)).
- **Separate keys by network.** Use different wallet addresses for Testnet and Mainnet ramp integrations. Never reuse Testnet keys for production transactions ([Security Best Practices](https://docs.sui.io/develop/security/best-practices)).
- **Validate callback data.** If the provider sends webhook callbacks or delivery confirmations, verify signatures and check data freshness before updating your app state. Define explicit failure behavior for missing, stale, or malformed callback data ([Security Best Practices](https://docs.sui.io/develop/security/best-practices)).
