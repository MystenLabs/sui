The `MarginPool` is a shared object that manages liquidity for a specific asset, enabling lenders to supply assets and margin traders to borrow them. Each margin pool tracks supply and borrow positions, accrues interest over time, and enforces risk parameters to maintain system health.

Margin pools use a shares-based accounting system where suppliers receive shares representing their proportion of the total supply. Interest accrues continuously, increasing the value of these shares over time. Borrowers can only borrow from pools that have enabled their specific DeepBook trading pool.

## What is a margin pool?

A margin pool is a shared, asset-specific liquidity contract on Sui. Lenders deposit assets into the pool and receive supply shares in return. Those shares increase in value as borrowers pay interest. Margin traders draw from the pool to fund leveraged positions on DeepBook.

Each pool enforces a set of risk parameters:

- **Supply cap:** The maximum total amount of assets the pool accepts.
- **Max utilization rate:** The maximum fraction of supplied assets that borrowers can use at one time. Keeping some assets liquid protects lenders who want to withdraw.
- **Min borrow:** The smallest borrow amount the pool permits, preventing dust positions.
- **Protocol spread:** The portion of interest income that goes to the protocol rather than suppliers.

## How does interest accrual work?

Interest accrues based on the pool's utilization rate, which is the ratio of total borrowed assets to total supplied assets. As utilization rises, the interest rate increases. This incentivizes lenders to supply more capital and discourages over-borrowing.

Suppliers do not receive interest as direct token transfers. Instead, the value of each supply share increases over time. When you withdraw, you receive more tokens than you originally deposited because your shares are worth more.

The protocol captures a portion of the interest income as fees, set by the pool's protocol spread. These fees accumulate separately and split between the protocol, the pool maintainer, and supply referrals: 25% to the protocol, 25% to the maintainer, and 50% to supply referrals pro rata to referred shares. When a pool has no referred shares, that last half goes to the protocol and the maintainer instead.

## How do borrowers access a margin pool?

A margin pool must explicitly allow a DeepBook trading pool before traders can borrow from it. The `deepbook_pool_allowed` field tracks which pools have permission. This design lets pool administrators control which markets can access the liquidity, limiting exposure to specific trading pairs.

## Common workflow

The typical workflow for a lender interacting with a margin pool:

1. Mint a `SupplierCap` using `mint_supplier_cap`. One cap works across multiple pools.
2. Call `supply` with your `SupplierCap` and the assets you want to deposit. The pool records your shares.
3. Monitor the pool's interest rate and utilization rate using the read endpoints.
4. Call `withdraw` when you want to reclaim your assets plus accrued interest. The `amount` parameter is an `Option<u64>` denominated in tokens, not shares. Pass `some(amount)` to take a specific amount, or `none` to withdraw everything the cap holds.

Two things can cap a withdrawal below what your shares are worth. The pool's withdrawal rate limiter aborts with `ERateLimitExceeded` when recent withdrawals have drained the bucket, so check `get_available_withdrawal` before you submit. The pool's vault also has to hold the assets: if borrowers have drawn them down, the call aborts with `ENotEnoughAssetInPool` until utilization falls.
