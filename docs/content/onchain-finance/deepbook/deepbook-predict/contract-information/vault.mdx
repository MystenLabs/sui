The Predict pool is the counterparty to every trade. Liquidity providers deposit DUSDC, receive PLP shares, and collectively back the payout liability of every active expiry. All of that capital sits in one shared object, `deepbook_predict::plp::PoolVault`.

Liquidity is asynchronous. Nothing mints or burns PLP at the moment a provider asks for it. A request joins a queue with a fill limit, and a later privileged flush values the whole pool once and drains both queues at that single frozen mark.

:::caution

Predict is available on Sui Testnet only. There is no Mainnet deployment, and DUSDC and PLP are Testnet assets with no market value.

:::

## The pool vault {#accounting-model}

`PoolVault` is created and shared at package publish, alongside the PLP currency registration. It holds five things:

| **Field** | **Type** | **Holds** |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | `UID` | The shared object identity. |
| `protocol_reserve_balance` | `Balance<DUSDC>` | Protocol-owned DUSDC, excluded from PLP redemption. No package call withdraws it. |
| `fee_incentive_reserve` | `Balance<DUSDC>` | Sponsor-funded DUSDC for taker fee subsidies, excluded from PLP NAV. |
| `lp` | `LpBook<PLP>` | The PLP treasury cap, both request queues with their escrow, and the locked bootstrap balance. |
| `expiry_accounting` | `Ledger` | Idle DUSDC custody, the active-expiry set, per-expiry cash-flow rows, and the profit basis. |

The pool owns no expiry-local state. Each `ExpiryMarket` owns its own trading cash, strike exposure, payout backing, and risk state. The pool coordinates capital across expiries and delegates every expiry-local invariant to the expiry itself.

### The LP book {#lp-book}

`lp_book` owns share issuance and both request queues. Every function in the module is `public(package)`, so no integrator calls it directly. Observe its state through the `PoolVault` accessors and the pool events instead.

A queue is a paged list. Each `RequestPage` holds up to 64 entries, and each `RequestEntry` records the queue index, the requesting account, the recipient address, the escrowed amount, the request's own minimum output, and how many flushes it has already missed.

- `plp_total_supply` includes the permanently locked bootstrap shares, which are never withdrawable.

## Asynchronous liquidity {#async-liquidity}

Four calls move liquidity in and out, and all four need nothing but account `Auth`. None of them is capability-gated, and none of them touches an oracle:

| **Call** | **Escrows** | **Limit argument** | **Returns** |
|---|---|---|---|
| `request_supply` | `amount` DUSDC pulled from account custody | `min_plp_out` | `u64` queue index |
| `request_withdraw` | `amount` PLP shares pulled from account custody | `min_dusdc_out` | `u64` queue index |
| `cancel_supply_request` | Refunds the escrowed DUSDC to the account | Takes the queue `index` | Nothing |
| `cancel_withdraw_request` | Refunds the escrowed PLP to the account | Takes the queue `index` | Nothing |

Both requests route through the account rather than the transaction signer, so a composing vault's own account receives the fill. The recipient recorded at request time is the account's `receive_address`, and fills and refunds are delivered to it through the balance accumulator.

Both limits are measured after the pool's supply or withdraw fee, so they bound what the account actually receives. Each is a price floor rather than a promise of a quantity: if the flush has room for only part of the request, the fill is proportionally smaller at the same price and the remainder stays queued with its limit rescaled. At the shipped `lp_request_limit_flush_attempts` of `1`, a flush whose mark quotes below the limit cancels and refunds the request on the spot rather than holding the queue head.

Both cancels are recipient-checked. The caller's account `receive_address` must equal the recipient recorded on the request, otherwise the call aborts with `lp_book::ENotRequestOwner`. A cancel only works while the request is still pending, and no call can run while a valuation is in progress.

Minimum sizes apply per request:

| **Action** | **Minimum** | **Raw** | **Abort** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supply | 10 DUSDC | `10_000_000` | `lp_book::EBelowMinSupplyRequest` |
| Withdraw | 1 PLP | `1_000_000` | `lp_book::EBelowMinWithdrawRequest` |

## The flush {#the-flush}

A flush values the entire pool once and then drains both queues against that one value. It has exactly three stages:

1. `start_pool_valuation` consumes a `MarketLifecycleProof` by value, engages the valuation lock on `ProtocolConfig`, snapshots the active expiry set, and returns a `PoolValuation`.
1. `value_expiry` runs once per snapshotted market. Each call rebalances that market's cash against the pool, then folds the market's value into a running total. A settled market is swept and contributes `0`, and a live market contributes its exact `current_nav`.
1. `finish_flush` consumes the `PoolValuation` by value, proves every snapshotted market was valued exactly once, computes the LP-attributable pool NAV, drains both queues at that mark, releases the lock, and returns the pool NAV as a `u64`.

Only the first stage is capability-gated. The proof comes from `registry::generate_lifecycle_proof`, which itself requires an allowlisted `MarketLifecycleCap`. The other two stages carry no capability: holding `&mut PoolValuation` or the potato itself is the authority, backed by an assertion that a valuation is in progress.

`PoolValuation` has no abilities, so it cannot be stored, transferred, or dropped, so the whole flush must be one programmable transaction block. The valuation lock is engaged when the potato is created and released inside `finish_flush`, so there is no way to leave the pool half-valued.

Settlement is a separate step. `value_expiry` loads a live pricer, and a live pricer cannot be built for a market that has crossed its expiry, so compose `expiry_market::try_settle` before the `value_expiry` command for any market whose settlement might be due. An expired unsettled market moves no cash and then aborts the valuation until settlement succeeds.

`finish_flush` also takes `supply_budget` and `withdraw_budget`, each an `Option<u64>` bounding how many requests that queue can process. `none` means unbounded. The budgets are independent, so a supply backlog can never starve withdrawals, and an operator sizes them against the gas left after valuing the snapshotted markets.

## One mark for both sides {#share-price}

`finish_flush` computes the LP-attributable pool NAV from the accumulated per-expiry total, then snapshots it once against the PLP supply:

```text
gross_pool_value = idle_balance + sum of each active expiry's current_nav
exclusion        = protocol_reserve_profit_share
                   * max(0, (profit_basis_credits + sum of current_nav) - profit_basis_debits) / 1e9
pool_nav         = max(0, gross_pool_value - exclusion - pending_protocol_profit)
```

Both subtracted terms are protocol profit that is not yet sitting in the reserve. `exclusion` is the protocol's share of profit that NAV has priced in but that has not yet materialized into cash. `pending_protocol_profit` is a cut that has materialized but whose cash could not be moved yet because idle was deployed elsewhere. The two are disjoint, and neither belongs to liquidity providers.

Each expiry's contribution is exact rather than estimated:

```text
current_nav = max(0, free_cash - live_marked_liability)
free_cash   = cash_balance - inventory_impact_reserve
```

`live_marked_liability` is the full payout-tree walk that prices each distinct boundary tick once through the resolved pricer, so every open position is worth exactly its quantity multiplied by its range probability. There is no verified-versus-unscanned bucket split, no uncertainty band, and no separate supply and withdraw pricing.

That single exact mark is what makes both directions fair:

- A supplier cannot dilute incumbents, because the mark never undercounts true recoverable value.
- A withdrawer cannot overdraw, because the mark never overcounts it.
- The mark cannot be timed against a self-supplied oracle update, because only a lifecycle-cap holder can start a flush and the potato can be created no other way.

Fees are charged on the DUSDC leg after the mark and never inside it, so they do not move the price either side trades at.

## Filling the queues {#redemption-value}

The drain runs supplies first, then withdrawals, each from the head of its queue in order. Because supplies run first, DUSDC supplied in a flush is available to pay that same flush's withdrawals.

Supply fills mint PLP against the frozen pair:

```text
fee    = ceil(amount * plp_supply_fee_rate / 1e9)
shares = floor((amount - fee) * total_supply / pool_nav)
```

Withdraw fills burn PLP and pay out of idle DUSDC:

```text
gross  = floor(shares * pool_nav / total_supply)
payout = gross - ceil(gross * plp_withdraw_fee_rate / 1e9)
```

The whole escrow is consumed in both directions. A supply joins its full DUSDC into idle including the fee, and a withdrawal burns its full escrowed PLP while the fee stays in idle.

Three things bound a pass beyond the operator budgets:

- **Pool value cap:** supplies fill only up to `max_lp_pool_value`, measured against the frozen mark plus the supplies already filled in this flush. A head larger than the remaining headroom fills to the headroom and keeps its remainder queued. The deployed value is `18446744073709551615`, the maximum of a `u64`, so the cap is present in the code path but not binding on this deployment. It is mutable protocol state, so read it before you rely on it staying inert.
- **Idle liquidity:** withdrawals fill only up to idle DUSDC. A head that idle cannot cover in full is paid as far as idle reaches, keeps its position, and stops the pass. Withdrawals are never reordered around a too-large head.
- **Request limits:** a head whose quote is below its own `min_plp_out` or `min_dusdc_out` is refunded at the shipped attempt count of `1`, and the pass continues. A head whose mark or quote is not executable at all is refunded rather than aborting the flush.

Cash already funded into an expiry is not directly redeemable until it returns through a rebalance or settlement, so a large exit can be bounded by idle and deferred. It cannot force-drain a live market.

## Worked example {#worked-example}

Assume these values at the moment `finish_flush` runs, with `plp_supply_fee_rate` at `0` and `plp_withdraw_fee_rate` at `2_000_000`, which is 0.2 percent:

| **Input** | **Raw value** | **Reads as** |
|---|---|---|
| `idle_balance` | `500_000_000_000` | 500,000 DUSDC |
| Sum of `current_nav` | `120_000_000_000` | 120,000 DUSDC |
| `profit_basis_credits` | `10_000_000_000` | 10,000 DUSDC |
| `profit_basis_debits` | `40_000_000_000` | 40,000 DUSDC |
| `pending_protocol_profit` | `0` | 0 DUSDC |
| `plp_total_supply` | `600_000_000_000` | 600,000 PLP |

The mark follows directly:

- **Gross pool value:** `500,000 + 120,000 = 620,000` DUSDC.
- **Exclusion:** `10% of max(0, 10,000 + 120,000 - 40,000) = 9,000` DUSDC.
- **Pool NAV:** `620,000 - 9,000 - 0 = 611,000` DUSDC.
- **Mark:** `611,000 / 600,000 = 1.018333` DUSDC per PLP.

A queued 1,000 DUSDC supply and a queued 10,000 PLP withdrawal then fill at that one mark:

- **Supply 1,000 DUSDC:** `floor(1_000_000_000 * 600_000_000_000 / 611_000_000_000) = 981_996_726`, which is 981.996726 PLP.
- **Withdraw 10,000 PLP, gross:** `floor(10_000_000_000 * 611_000_000_000 / 600_000_000_000) = 10_183_333_333`, which is 10,183.333333 DUSDC.
- **Withdrawal fee:** `ceil(10_183_333_333 * 2_000_000 / 1_000_000_000) = 20_366_667`, which is 20.366667 DUSDC.
- **Withdraw payout:** `10_183_333_333 - 20_366_667 = 10_162_966_666`, which is 10,162.966666 DUSDC.

## Funding a market {#rebalance}

A freshly created `ExpiryMarket` holds zero DUSDC and is not mintable. Minting asserts backing but never pulls pool cash, so the call that makes a market tradable is:

```move title='packages/predict/sources/plp/plp.move'
public fun rebalance_expiry_cash(
    vault: &mut PoolVault,
    market: &mut ExpiryMarket,
    config: &ProtocolConfig,
    clock: &Clock,
)
```

`rebalance_expiry_cash` is permissionless and standalone. Anyone can call it at any cadence, and the same inner logic runs inside `value_expiry` during a flush. It handles all three per-market cases: initial funding of an unfunded market, ongoing top-up or surplus sweep of a live market, and the terminal sweep of a settled one.

The policy is a band around each expiry's required cash, with hysteresis so that small moves do not thrash cash back and forth:

```text
required_cash    = payout_liability + inventory_impact_reserve
target_cash      = max(required_cash * (1 + band), initial_expiry_cash)
sweep_threshold  = max(required_cash * (1 + 2 * band), initial_expiry_cash)
```

`band` is `expiry_rebalance_pct`, which is `100_000_000` at 1e9 scale, or 10 percent. Below `target_cash` the pool tops the market up, and above `sweep_threshold` it pulls the excess back to idle.

Both floors are the market's own `initial_expiry_cash`, snapshotted from its cadence when the market was created, not one protocol-wide constant. The floor therefore differs by cadence:

| **Cadence** | **`initial_expiry_cash` raw** | **Reads as** |
|---|---|---|
| 1m | `10_000_000_000` | 10,000 DUSDC |
| 5m | `10_000_000_000` | 10,000 DUSDC |
| 1h | `50_000_000_000` | 50,000 DUSDC |
| 1d | `50_000_000_000` | 50,000 DUSDC |
| 1w | `50_000_000_000` | 50,000 DUSDC |

`constants::expiry_cash_floor!()`, which is `10_000_000_000`, plays no part in the rebalance. It is the minimum admissible `initial_expiry_cash` that cadence validation enforces, so a cadence configured below 10,000 DUSDC aborts with `EInvalidCadenceConfig`. A market on a cadence configured above that minimum floors its rebalance at the configured figure, not at 10,000 DUSDC. Read the live figures with `registry::cadence_config`, and see the cadence table on [Contract Information](/onchain-finance/deepbook/deepbook-predict/contract-information#cadences).

Two limits bound a top-up: available idle DUSDC, and the expiry's remaining funding room. Funding room is net of returns, and the cap is the `max_expiry_allocation` snapshotted from the cadence configuration when the market was created. On Testnet that is 50,000 DUSDC for the 1m and 5m cadences and 250,000 DUSDC for 1h, 1d, and 1w. Exceeding it aborts with `pool_accounting::EMaxExpiryFundingExceeded`.

Call `expiry_market::try_settle` first in the same transaction when settlement might be due. An expired unsettled market is a no-op for the rebalance.

## Per-expiry backing {#max-payout}

Solvency is enforced per expiry, not pool-wide. Each market's cash leaf, `expiry_cash::ExpiryCash`, asserts on every cash movement that:

```text
cash_balance >= payout_liability + inventory_impact_reserve
```

For a live market, `payout_liability` is a settlement floor plus a liquidity buffer:

```text
payout_liability = max_net_payout
                   + backing_buffer_lambda * (sum of net_payout - max_net_payout) / 1e9
```

`max_net_payout` is the largest summed net payout at any single settlement price. Exactly one price settles a market, so that floor alone covers every possible settlement outcome in full. The buffer adds `backing_buffer_lambda`, which the Testnet template sets to `310_000_000` or 31 percent, of the gap between that floor and the sum of every open order's maximum payout. The buffer is what funds early exits of positions that do not overlap the book's worst-case price point. A live redeem that would push cash below the requirement aborts with `expiry_cash::EInsufficientCash`, and the holder can close a smaller quantity, retry after the next rebalance, and is always paid in full at settlement.

The `inventory_impact_reserve` is an isolated escrow of inventory-impact charges collected at mint, minus rebates paid to voluntary live closes. It is excluded from NAV and from pool sweeps while the market is live, and settlement releases the residual because no live close can earn another rebate.

How each case is treated:

- Receiving cash: joins the funds without re-checking backing, because receiving cash can only improve it.
- Releasing surplus: requires cash to cover required backing plus the released amount, so a sweep can never break solvency.
- Settled release: computes the terminal liability at the settlement price, asserts backing, and returns only the strict excess.

Read the live figures from the market:

```move title='packages/predict/sources/expiry_market.move'

```

## Bootstrap, sponsorship, and capacity {#initialization-and-access-control}

Three operations sit outside the ordinary supply and withdraw path:

| **Operation** | **Call** | **Authorization** |
|---|---|---|
| Bootstrap the pool once | `lock_capital` | `AdminCap`, and only while total supply is zero |
| Sponsor taker fee incentives | `sponsor_fee_incentives` | Permissionless |
| Start a flush | `start_pool_valuation` | An allowlisted `MarketLifecycleCap`, through a `MarketLifecycleProof` |

`lock_capital` permanently locks a minimum of 10 DUSDC, mints matching PLP one to one into the book's locked balance, and joins the DUSDC into idle. The caller receives no shares. Locking keeps total supply above zero for the life of the vault and gives rounding dust a holder that can never withdraw. Supply, withdraw, and valuation flows all abort with `ENotBootstrapped` until it has run, and a second call aborts with `EAlreadyBootstrapped`.

`sponsor_fee_incentives` accepts DUSDC from anyone, with a minimum of 10 DUSDC. The payment joins the pool-level fee incentive reserve, which is excluded from PLP NAV and allocated out to expiry markets by the ordinary rebalance flow. Read the pool-level balance with `fee_incentive_reserve` and the per-market balance with `expiry_market::fee_incentive_balance`.

Capacity is bounded when a market registers, not when it trades. Registering a market whose expiry is still ahead of the clock aborts with `EMaxLiveExpiryMarketsExceeded` once 24 live pre-expiry markets are already registered to the vault. That ceiling bounds how many `value_expiry` commands one flush can need. Settled or expired markets still sitting in the active set do not count against it, because they contribute `0` rather than a live valuation walk.

| `RequestLimitMissed` | `pool_vault_id`, `account_id`, `recipient`, `index`, `amount`, `is_supply`, `quoted_output`, `min_output`, `missed_flushes`, `max_misses` |
| `SupplyFilled` | `pool_vault_id`, `account_id`, `recipient`, `index`, `dusdc_amount`, `shares_minted`, `fee_dusdc`, `dusdc_remaining`, `requests_pending_after` |
| `WithdrawFilled` | `pool_vault_id`, `account_id`, `recipient`, `index`, `shares_burned`, `dusdc_amount`, `fee_dusdc`, `shares_remaining`, `requests_pending_after` |
| `FlushExecuted` | `pool_vault_id`, `epoch`, `pool_value`, `total_supply`, `supply_fee_rate`, `withdraw_fee_rate`, `active_market_nav`, `market_count`, `idle_balance_before`, `supplies_filled`, `withdrawals_filled`, `requests_processed`, `idle_balance_after`, `total_supply_after` |
| `CapitalLocked` | `pool_vault_id`, `amount` |
| `FeeIncentivesSponsored` | `pool_vault_id`, `sponsor`, `amount`, `reserve_after` |
| `FeeIncentivesAllocated` | `pool_vault_id`, `expiry_market_id`, `amount`, `pool_reserve_after`, `expiry_incentive_balance_after`, `expiry_incentives_allocated_after` |
| `FeeIncentivesReturned` | `pool_vault_id`, `expiry_market_id`, `amount`, `pool_reserve_after` |

`RequestCancelled.reason` distinguishes why an escrow came back:

| **Value** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|
| `0` | The account cancelled the request. |
| `1` | The flush found the mark or quote not executable. |
| `2` | The request missed its own output limit. |

One `FlushExecuted` per flush carries the priced mark and its breakdown, so `pool_value` divided by `total_supply` is the exact mark that flush's fills used.

## Read and write the pool from a client {#read-vault-state-from-a-client}

`@mysten/deepbook-v3` version 2.1.3 or later exposes the LP path on its `/predict` subpath. Every read runs against the Sui client's core API, with no indexer or server in the path.

The four queue builders mirror the Move calls:

| **Call** | **Signature** | **Units** |
|---|---|---|
| Queue a supply | `client.predict.tx.supplyPlp(owner, amountUsdc)` | USD decimal, `number` or `string` |
| Queue a withdrawal | `client.predict.tx.withdrawPlp(owner, shares)` | Raw PLP shares as a `bigint`, not USD |
| Cancel a supply | `client.predict.tx.cancelSupplyPlp(owner, index)` | `bigint` queue index |
| Cancel a withdrawal | `client.predict.tx.cancelWithdrawPlp(owner, index)` | `bigint` queue index |

`withdrawPlp` taking raw shares is the sharpest edge on this surface. Read the balance to withdraw with `client.predict.read.plpBalance(owner)`, which also returns a raw `bigint`, and pass it straight through. The facade pins `min_plp_out` and `min_dusdc_out` to `0`, so it surfaces no per-request floor.

Recover the queue index from the request transaction with the decoder, which parses event BCS locally and makes no network call:

```ts
const req = client.predict.decode.plpRequest(supplyResult);
req.kind;   // 'supply' | 'withdraw'
req.index;  // bigint - pass to cancelSupplyPlp or cancelWithdrawPlp
```

`client.predict.read.pool()` returns a `PoolSummary`. Note what the last two fields are:

| **Field** | **Type** | **Meaning** |
|---|---|---|
| `plpTotalSupply` | `bigint` | Raw PLP supply. |
| `idleUsdc` | `number` | Idle DUSDC as a USD decimal. |
| `supplyRequestsPending` | `number` | Number of queued supply requests, not their total amount. |
| `withdrawRequestsPending` | `number` | Number of queued withdraw requests, not their total amount. |

## Error codes {#error-codes}

`plp` raises these:

| **Code** | **Constant** | **Cause** |
|---|---|---|
| `0` | `EExpiryMarketNotActive` | `value_expiry` was called for a market outside the start snapshot. |
| `1` | `EExpiryMarketAlreadyValued` | The same market was valued twice in one valuation. |
| `2` | `EWrongPoolVault` | `finish_flush` was handed a valuation from another vault. |
| `3` | `EMissingExpiryValuation` | The valued set does not match the snapshot at finish. |
| `4` | `ENotBootstrapped` | A supply, withdraw, or valuation ran before `lock_capital`. |
| `5` | `EAlreadyBootstrapped` | `lock_capital` was called a second time. |
| `6` | `EBelowMinBootstrapLiquidity` | The `lock_capital` payment is below 10 DUSDC. |
| `7` | `EBelowMinFeeIncentiveSponsorship` | The sponsorship is below 10 DUSDC. |
| `8` | `EMaxLiveExpiryMarketsExceeded` | Registering the market would exceed 24 live pre-expiry markets. |

`lp_book` raises these through the queue calls:

| **Code** | **Constant** | **Cause** |
|---|---|---|
| `0` | `ERequestNotFound` | The index is absent, the queue is empty, or a partial fill equals the full amount. |
| `1` | `EBelowMinSupplyRequest` | The supply is below 10 DUSDC. |
| `2` | `EBelowMinWithdrawRequest` | The withdrawal is below 1 PLP. |
| `3` | `ENotRequestOwner` | The cancelling account is not the recorded recipient. |

`pool_accounting` and `expiry_cash` raise these through cash movement:

| **Code** | **Constant** | **Module** | **Cause** |
|---|---|---|---|
| `0` | `EUnknownRegisteredExpiry` | `pool_accounting` | The expiry ID is not registered to this vault. |
| `1` | `ERegisteredExpiryAlreadyExists` | `pool_accounting` | The expiry was registered twice. |
| `2` | `EMaxExpiryFundingExceeded` | `pool_accounting` | Funding would exceed the expiry's `max_expiry_allocation`. |
| `3` | `ETerminalAccountingStarted` | `pool_accounting` | Live-path accounting ran after terminal accounting began. |
| `0` | `EInsufficientCash` | `expiry_cash` | The backing check failed, or a release or payment exceeds the balance. |
| `1` | `EInventoryImpactRebateExceedsReserve` | `expiry_cash` | A rebate exceeds the isolated escrow. |

A flush also runs into `ProtocolConfig` gates: `EValuationNotInProgress` when a stage runs without the lock, `EValuationInProgress` when a NAV-changing flow runs while it is held, and `EPackageVersionDisabled` or `EProtocolFrozen` when the package version or freeze switch blocks the call.

Error numbers repeat across modules, so resolve an abort against the module named in the abort location rather than against the number alone. For market creation, cadence configuration, and the lifecycle capability, see [Registry](/onchain-finance/deepbook/deepbook-predict/contract-information/registry). For the accounts these calls route through, see [Accounts and Custody](/onchain-finance/deepbook/deepbook-predict/contract-information/predict-manager).
